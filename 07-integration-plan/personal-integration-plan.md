# Personal AI Integration Plan

**Role:** Business Analyst

This plan describes realistic ways I can use generative AI in Business Analysis work while keeping accuracy, confidentiality, and final decisions under human control.

| Recurring Task | Expected Benefit | Preferred Prompt or Workflow | Main Risk / Verification Requirement | Frequency | What Success Looks Like |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Turn stakeholder meeting notes into a decision-ready brief | Saves time and makes actions, decisions, and open questions easier to track | Use the **Meeting Summary** prompt and follow Input → Prompt → AI Output → Human Review → Final Output | AI may invent owners, deadlines, or decisions. Compare names, dates, actions, and decisions with the original notes | After relevant project meetings | A concise brief is ready quickly, key facts match the source, and no invented information remains |
| Draft stakeholder updates and professional emails | Improves clarity, consistency, and tone while reducing drafting time | Use the **Stakeholder Communication** prompt followed by Draft → Verify → Refine → Human Sign-off | Incorrect dates, unconfirmed impacts, unsuitable tone, or accidental disclosure. Verify facts and remove sensitive information | As needed, typically one to three times per week | The final message is accurate, concise, audience-appropriate, and approved before sending |
| Organise and prioritise business requirements | Makes requirements easier to review and highlights gaps or conflicting priorities | Use the **Requirements Extractor** followed by the **Requirements Prioritiser** workflow | AI may add requirements or assumptions that stakeholders did not provide. Trace important items back to source evidence and obtain human approval | During each requirements cycle | Requirements are traceable, gaps are visible, priorities are justified, and stakeholders confirm the final version |

## How I Will Use AI After Training

1. Start with a clear C.A.R.E. or R.C.T.O. prompt instead of a vague request.
2. Use fictional, public, or properly anonymised information unless an approved tool and organisational policy allow otherwise.
3. Keep the original source available while reviewing the AI output.
4. Mark missing information as `[Not Specified]` rather than allowing AI to guess.
5. Check facts, tone, assumptions, and potential bias before sharing any output.
6. Keep human approval as the final step for important communications, requirements, recommendations, and decisions.
7. Save useful prompts and improve them based on verified results.
