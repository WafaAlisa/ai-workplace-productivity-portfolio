# Verification Checklist

## My Personal Checklist

Before using or sharing AI-generated workplace content, I will:

1. Identify the type of content, intended audience, and purpose.
2. Compare names, dates, numbers, decisions, requirements, and actions with the original source.
3. Check that uncertainty, assumptions, and missing information are clearly labelled.
4. Confirm that the AI did not invent owners, deadlines, statuses, decisions, or requirements.
5. Check that the format, tone, and level of detail are appropriate for the intended audience.
6. Remove or anonymise confidential, private, or sensitive information.
7. Record important corrections and obtain human approval before final use.

### Application of the Checklist

| Check | What I Tested | Finding | Action Taken |
|---|---|---|---|
| Content and purpose | Decision-ready Business Analysis brief | The format was appropriate, but some statements were too certain | Rewrote the brief to preserve uncertainty |
| Names and dates | Wafa and the 18 September deadline | These matched the source | Kept them unchanged |
| Decisions vs. suggestions | Central service portal | AI incorrectly presented the portal as a confirmed decision | Changed it to a proposed option |
| Missing owners and deadlines | Business Analyst deadline and IT deadline | These were not provided in the source | Replaced them with `[Not Specified]` |
| Status | Wafa's action and other activities | Status information was not provided | Removed unsupported status claims |
| Requirements | Request tracking and access controls | Some details were presented as confirmed requirements when they were only discussed | Reworded them as points requiring clarification |
| Sensitive information | Employee information mentioned in the notes | The scenario is fictional and contains no real confidential data | Confirmed safe for this portfolio |
| Human approval | Final structured output | Corrections were reviewed against the raw notes | Marked final version as trainee-reviewed |

## Verification Result

The original AI output was **not ready to use** because it treated proposed ideas as confirmed decisions and invented or implied information that was not provided in the source.

After comparing the output with the raw notes, I corrected the unsupported statements and produced a final brief that clearly distinguishes confirmed information, proposed ideas, open questions, and missing information.

**Final decision:** Approved for inclusion as a fictional, trainee-reviewed course example.
